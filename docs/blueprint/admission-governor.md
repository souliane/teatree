# BLUEPRINT Appendix — Adaptive admission governor and machine pressure

Detail behind [BLUEPRINT.md](https://github.com/souliane/teatree/blob/main/BLUEPRINT.md) §5.6 ("Adaptive admission governor"). The rule it implements: static concurrency settings are ceilings, and every dispatcher asks one pure governor before admitting work.

A pending task holds its five-minute admission seat only while it is due (`not_before` is empty or past). A checkout-fence park clears the admission stamp, so the task remains unseated when its retry becomes due; usage-window and route-failure parks preserve the stamp for the cycle-time timeline and the five-minute re-admit guard. A checkout-fence park that frees a seat schedules a new admission walk after commit. Other future parks free their lane seat while parked but wait for the periodic drain to walk admission. The walk admits due, unseated pending tasks only when the ticket has no held Worktree row, except a finished task holder with no recorded owner that checkout acquisition can self-heal. Releasing a holder schedules the walk after commit, including for never-parked siblings; holder-scoped checkout parks are re-armed at once. A due park retains the usual five-minute duplicate-dispatch guard. The cheap-lane released-seat warning excludes future parked rows. The periodic drain uses the same held-ticket filter, so an unknown-overlay failure on a held ticket is deferred until its hold releases or lapses.

The periodic architectural review has one reserved seat outside both the coding and cheap-class ceilings. It keeps the pressure HALT and SHED brakes; a due task is admitted by the ordinary post-save walk, then runs on its own `cheap` executor. A failed credit attempt gets one plans-only retry, while terminal outcomes and governor holds issue one owner receipt per task.

## The governor

**Adaptive admission governor — static concurrency settings are CEILINGS, not targets ([#3644](https://github.com/souliane/teatree/issues/3644)).** Concurrency was a hand-set constant whose only feedback loop was a human watching load. `core/admission_governor.py` is the pure, model-free decision every dispatcher consults BEFORE admitting work; `loop/admission.py` assembles its signals, persists the brake state and logs the refusal; `loop_dispatch._admit_budget_exhausted` is the loop-lane admission chokepoint; and `src/teatree/agents/_runner_env.with_test_worker_cap` applies the test-worker half. **Every dispatcher means the INTERACTIVE one too ([#4107](https://github.com/souliane/teatree/issues/4107)).** The governor had exactly two callers, both factory lanes, so an orchestrator session dispatching through the harness `Agent`/`Task` tool was admitted with no ceiling and no load brake — the guards governed the headless population while the agent population on the box is the SUM of both (measured at load 58 on 8 cores with 1 GB free while `issue_implementer_max_concurrent = 3` was in force). `core/dispatch_admission.py` is the third caller, driven by `hooks/scripts/dispatch_admission_gate.py` on the `PreToolUse` `Agent`/`Task` gate — the ONE interception point a dispatch has ([#4216](https://github.com/souliane/teatree/issues/4216)). The task-LIST tools are a separate family: their `TaskCreated` event has one producer, the `TaskCreate` tool body, so no dispatch reaches it, a todo puts no agent on the box, and nothing there is seated or braked. The gate's ceiling is compared against the TOTAL live-agent population (`active_claims`, both execution targets), since a new interactive agent lands on the same box; a sub-agent's ONWARD dispatch skips the ceiling — its own lane already admitted it and its own claim is counted in that ceiling, so re-clamping would deadlock it against itself — while the brakes, which read box- and account-wide signals, still apply. **The ceiling counts the population it admits ([#4129](https://github.com/souliane/teatree/issues/4129)).** `active_claims` is durable and the right shape, but an ad-hoc interactive dispatch creates no `Task` row at all — the very scenario this lane exists for — so the ceiling could not see its own admissions and N rapid dispatches each read the same live count and every one passed, leaving only the brakes, which fire *after* the box is saturated. `core/models/interactive_dispatch.InteractiveDispatch` is this lane's `Task.admitted_at`: a seat taken AT the gate and counted by `live_agent_count` alongside the claims. As in #4125 the bound is arbitrated INSIDE the write — the seat is inserted first and its own rank among the live seats re-read, so a racer loses on its rank rather than on a count that never saw it, and a refused seat is deleted so a denial never narrows the lane it was refused by. The seat's ordinary end is `hooks/scripts/dispatch_seat_release.handle_subagent_stop_release` on `SubagentStop`, keyed on the terminating `agent_id` for idempotency (the seat predates the id, so no seat can be bound to its agent at birth); `SEAT_WINDOW` (30 minutes) is the BACKSTOP for a release that never arrives — a dispatch that never materialised, a killed agent, a harness restart — not the expected lifetime, since a seat that only ever lapsed on its window would bound a dispatch RATE rather than a population. The ceiling-EXEMPT onward dispatch still takes a seat: the exemption is from being CLAMPED, never from being COUNTED, so that path is at least visible to the arm that does clamp instead of the two gaps compounding. The hook adapter stays fail-open and now covers the ledger too: an unreadable signal or a seat write that raises admits the dispatch and says SKIPPED on stderr, while the core function propagates the failure so an unmeasured call never reads as a measured admission. The per-action `[gate-escape:dispatch_admission: <reason>]` escape and `_fail_open_or_deny` routing remain available. **Token budget is the PRIMARY signal, machine load secondary** — the budget that actually runs out is the model quota on a WEEKLY window, so an idle box with a spent weekly window admits nothing. The quota signal reads the `AnthropicTokenUsage` health cache the routing selector already maintains (per-account 5h + 7d utilization, 7d reset, exhaustion) — no network probe, no model — and `decide_admission` evaluates quota brakes → machine brake → yield brake. Pacing is weekly, not a sprint to zero: `weekly_pace = headroom / runway`, and below 1 the ceiling scales down to land AT the reset, below `PACE_DENY` the governor stops admitting; an unknown reset reads as a FULL window (the conservative direction). The yield brake counts terminal `Task` outcomes over a 6h window and STOPS admitting below a 20% completion ratio with ≥5 samples, because the marginal token is buying zero; fewer than 5 samples is UNKNOWN and never brakes. Machine pressure has hysteresis (deny at the box-global `admission_load_brake_per_core × cores` watermark (shipped 2.0), re-admit below `admission_load_resume_per_core × cores` (shipped 1.5)) with the previous brake state riding in the existing `tick-meta.json` sidecar, and the ceiling floors at 1 so the factory cannot deadlock itself. Failure is safe by construction, and "safe" means BOUNDED, not unbounded ([#4097](https://github.com/souliane/teatree/issues/4097)): an `AdmissionDecision` always carries a positive `ceiling`, so an unknown budget can never buy more concurrency than a known-healthy one. A quota probe that reads successfully but reports `fresh=False` — the steady state, since healthy `AnthropicTokenUsage` rows expire in `HEALTH_TTL` minutes and are written only reactively — drops the weekly-pace scaling (the only part that needs the quota) and bounds the lane on `floor(cores × admission_write_concurrency_per_core)`, which needs no quota information at all; it never invents a tighter constant, because a clamp on evidence the governor does not have is the silent-starvation failure it exists to prevent. "No opinion" is the ABSENCE of a decision, not a decision with an absent ceiling: a probe that RAISES makes `governor_verdict` return `None` so the caller keeps its pre-governor static behaviour — never a denial. The headless lane matters here because it passes `static_ceiling=None` (no operator cap for that lane), so it is entirely dependent on the governor deriving one. Refusals are never silent: every `AdmissionDecision` carries a non-empty `reason` and `governor_verdict` logs a denial at WARNING with it, pinned by a test rather than by convention. The test-worker multiplier is the meltdown fix: `per_agent_test_workers` divides a `cores * TOTAL_TEST_WORKERS_PER_CORE` request budget by the active-agent count and exports it as `PYTEST_XDIST_AUTO_NUM_WORKERS`, which is how pytest-xdist resolves `-n auto` — the `pyproject` addopts are untouched, and the shared pool caps even a lone run at the box budget, while 12 agents × 8 auto-detected workers at load ~70 can no longer happen. **The bound reads MEMORY too, the resource that actually saturates ([#4163](https://github.com/souliane/teatree/issues/4163)).** `MachineSignal.ram_available_gb` was declared and populated by nothing — every live caller of `read_machine_signal` passed no argument — so the former `cores * 2` total of 16 workers was 10.4 GB at the measured p50 worker RSS but **19.8 GB at the p90 against 19.7 GB usable**: safe at the median, over the line at the tail, which is why the OOMs were relentless but intermittent (1374 kills in a fortnight, ~80% pytest-shaped). The fix is two-sided. `read_machine_signal` populates the field from `teatree.utils.ram_scope.read_ram_headroom` — the ONE cgroup-aware reader (`min(host MemAvailable, cgroup headroom)`), never a second `/proc/meminfo` read, which inside a container reports the HOST figure and would be invisible to that reader's own fix ([#4118](https://github.com/souliane/teatree/issues/4118)); its `None` (unreadable) stays a different answer from `0` (nothing left). **The reading carries the SCOPE it was taken in, because the watermarks below are absolute ([#4217](https://github.com/souliane/teatree/issues/4217)).** The same code returned opposite verdicts depending only on where it ran: the 2 GiB admin sidecar — where interactive work runs and every `docker exec` lands — read 1.65 GB at the instant the worker read 15.88 GB with 22 GB free on the box, so every `Agent`/`Task` dispatch made from it was denied, permanently, against a 4 GB floor a fixed 2 GiB cap can never rise above, under a deny text advising the operator to wait for hysteresis that could never arrive. `RamHeadroom` therefore carries `cgroup_limit_mib` beside the number, and `box_watermark_mib` drops the cgroup arithmetic whenever that cap sits below the agent-workload floor `t3 doctor`'s worker-cap FAIL already defines (`agent_workload_floor_gib`, one definition, same `TEATREE_WORKER_MEMORY_FLOOR_GIB` override). **Dropping the cgroup arm is not the same as having no answer ([#4252](https://github.com/souliane/teatree/issues/4252)).** `/proc/meminfo` inside a container reports the HOST, so the sidecar holds a box-scoped reading the whole time — and `read_ram_headroom` collapsed host and cgroup into `min()` *before* the scope test, making it unrecoverable, so the abstention fired on a genuinely starved box too: at 500 MiB free the brake stayed silent, the ceiling went unreduced and `per_agent_test_workers` skipped its memory clamp. `RamHeadroom` therefore carries `host_available_mib` as its own component, and an out-of-scope reading falls back to it. `None` — UNKNOWN, which never brakes — survives only when nothing box-scoped was readable at all, never as the verdict on a scope; the remedy is deliberately NOT making callers fail closed on `None`, which would restore exactly the permanent lockout #4217 fixed. So the uncapped host and a worker-sized cgroup are judged on `min(host, cgroup)`, and a sub-floor cgroup on the host figure alone. The second half is what "available" MEANS: cgroup-v2's `memory.current` charges page cache the kernel hands straight back, so `memory.max - memory.current` counted reclaimable cache as spent — measured under-reporting by ~1.4 GiB on the worker, and trending to near-zero after a day of `-n auto` suites, at which point the brake denies everything, `adapt_concurrency` clamps intake to 1 and `resource_pressure` DMs a CRITICAL, all on a box with tens of GB genuinely free. `cgroup_headroom_mib` credits `inactive_file + slab_reclaimable` from `memory.stat` — the cgroup mirror of what `MemAvailable` already does host-side, which is why the two arms of one function disagreed — and `resource_pressure._read_cgroup_headroom_gb` DELEGATES to it rather than restating the arithmetic, so the two readers cannot drift again. Three consumers read it, and no fourth: `per_agent_test_workers` takes the smaller of the cores-derived TOTAL and `floor((ram - RAM_BRAKE_FLOOR_GB) / test_worker_ram_gb)` — applied to the TOTAL, never the per-agent share, since a per-agent memory budget would hand each of N agents the whole box; the machine brake (`admission_pressure._machine_components`) carries a RAM watermark beside the load one (`RAM_BRAKE_FLOOR_GB = 4.0`, `RAM_RESUME_FLOOR_GB = 6.0`), sharing the load watermark's hysteresis and cheap-lane exemption (`MachineBrake`); and `resume_agent_ceiling` scales by `min(load headroom, memory headroom)`, because the recorded restore was load 58 **and** 1 GB free and a bound reading only load calls that half healthy. It is deliberately NOT a term in `admission_ceiling`: `adapt_concurrency` already owns "how many agents fit in memory", and two unsynchronised readers of one quantity drift ([#4125](https://github.com/souliane/teatree/issues/4125)) — a brake answers a different question. **The concurrency decision reads the WHOLE BOX, not only the factory's own count ([#4407](https://github.com/souliane/teatree/issues/4407)).** Load and memory are box-wide, but the counts every lane sizes itself against — `claimed_agent_count`, the intake in-flight budget — are lane-local by construction: an orchestrating session's harness sub-agents claim no `Task` and hold no intake marker, yet each runs a test suite on the same cores. So `adapt_concurrency` had exactly one box-wide input (memory) and sized intake off a number that excluded most of what the box was doing — measured, a fan-out took load 14 → 53 and 16 GB → 1 GB free while the factory correctly held intake at 3, every chip green and zero merges for over an hour. `box_load_headroom(load1, cores)` is the ONE whole-box occupancy reading — the fraction of the `BRAKE_LOAD_PER_CORE * cores` budget still free, `1.0` idle, `0.0` at the watermark the brake denies on, and `1.0` again for an unreadable probe, since a probe that cannot answer must never lower a ceiling. `resume_agent_ceiling` consumes it (it had computed the identical expression inline) and `adapt_concurrency` now takes it beside the memory term, the smaller of the two capacity estimates winning; `in_flight` is renamed `factory_in_flight`, because a parameter that reads as the whole box and means one lane is what made the gap easy to miss. **Foreign load BRAKES the producer and only REPORTS on the rest** — the trade the issue asks be decided rather than defaulted. Braking intake cannot deadlock the factory, because the review and ship lanes that DRAIN the pile are untouched (the same rationale that keeps `MergeSignal` out of `decide_admission`), and the term floors at `MIN_CONCURRENCY`, so a busy box tightens intake toward one slot and can never wedge it to zero. The agent lanes deliberately get no graded load-scaled ceiling: `floor(cores * WRITE_CONCURRENCY_PER_CORE)` is 2 on a 4-core box, so scaling it would leave ONE expensive slot and starve the drain — the factory-starves-itself outage that is its own incident. The reporting half is `t3 doctor check`'s `_check_box_occupancy`, which prints the factory's agent count beside the box's load on EVERY run, because the actionable thing is the difference between them and a check that speaks only when unhappy can never show one; it WARNs at the brake watermark and never FAILs, since foreign load is a fact about the machine rather than a fault in the factory. Managing non-factory processes stays out of scope — the ask is only that a decision about how much the box can take is not made from a number that excludes most of it. Failure is BOUNDED, not closed, the #4101 guard restated: an unreadable probe (or a non-positive per-worker size) yields exactly today's CPU-derived bound, the brake does not fire, and resume stays load-derived — never a denial and never a manufactured clamp to 1, because denying on an unreadable `/proc` file is a kill switch operated by a missing file. The p90 is a setting, not a magic number: `test_worker_ram_gb` (`UserSettings`, DB-home, per-overlay overridable, default **1.25**), derived from the kernel OOM log 2026-07-21..08-04 (p50 0.65 / p90 1.24 / max 23.1 GB — the p90 is the sizing point; the max is one pathological run, not a budget). The pure function reads no config: `src/teatree/agents/_runner_env.with_test_worker_cap` resolves the setting and passes it in. One trade-off stated rather than left silent: the cheap lane is exempt from the RAM brake while still consuming memory — bounded by its W+1 cheap seats plus the worker cap, which is NOT exempt. The governor runs on every admission path; its pressure thresholds remain configurable. **Concurrency ceiling ([#4069](https://github.com/souliane/teatree/issues/4069)).** The box-global setting `admission_write_concurrency_per_core` sizes it and ships at `WRITE_CONCURRENCY_PER_CORE = 0.5` (8 cores → 4). It was 0.25 (→ 2), calibrated against the meltdown recorded on `TOTAL_TEST_WORKERS_PER_CORE`, which names its own cause: *the per-agent expansion is the melt driver, not the agent count* (12 agents × 8 auto-detected workers ≈ 96 workers at load ~70). That driver is now bounded independently by `per_agent_test_workers`, which divides a `cores * TOTAL_TEST_WORKERS_PER_CORE` request budget by the active-agent count; the shared slot pool enforces the total across runs — the old value priced agent count as if it were the hazard and predated the guard. Because `decide_admission` combines the operator's static ceiling with the adaptive one using `min`, this setting — not `boost_concurrency` or `issue_implementer_max_concurrent` — is the lever on the agent ceiling of every lane that asks `decide_admission`: the headless verdict, the loop claim budget and the interactive dispatch gate. Above `HOST_AGENT_POPULATION_PER_CORE` the dispatch lanes admit more agents than the session-resume advisory treats as a survivable host population. Intake keeps its own `HARD_CAP_PER_CORE = 0.5` (`core/intake/concurrency.py`), so a raised factor widens the agent lanes, not how many tickets intake claims. Its default and range live once, as `WRITE_CONCURRENCY_PER_CORE` / `_MIN` / `_MAX` in `config/settings.py`: a write outside the range is refused with the bounds named (`config/write_validation.py`), a row that predates that check is clamped on read, and an unreadable or non-finite value keeps the default. It is box-global because its reader sizes this box's cores and resolves no overlay, so an overlay-scoped row is refused at write. `admission_ceiling` (`core/admission/write_ceiling.py`) returns the `AdmissionCeiling` (cores, factor, weekly pace) the decision is taken on, and `LaneWidths` (`core/admission/lanes.py`) gives the worker the seats it sizes its executor pools from on every supervisor poll (`headless_lane_widths`), so a raised factor raises how many agents actually run and an admitted seat never lapses in the queue waiting for a thread. `t3 worker status` resolves the verdict's own read (`headless_admission_status`: the same probe and claim-admission block) and prints the ceiling derivation, the lane widths, the occupancy, the pressure band and each class's denial (`agent_admission` in `--json`; an unreadable admission reports a one-line `unavailable` and `null` without failing the status), so the operator can read why the factory admits N. The weekly-pace scaling and the pressure brake apply unchanged at every factor. The raise is safe to ATTEMPT rather than safe by assertion: the load brake still denies above `BRAKE_LOAD_PER_CORE * cores` and holds to `RESUME_LOAD_PER_CORE * cores`, so an over-aggressive value throttles itself instead of melting the box. **Merge back-pressure — do not deepen a pile that cannot land ([#4044](https://github.com/souliane/teatree/issues/4044)).** The yield brake above asks whether TASKS finish, and a task that finishes by opening an unmergeable PR answers yes, so unmergeable inventory reads as success — four PRs sat stuck for a day while the implementer kept claiming more. `MergeSignal` adds the question yield cannot answer: is any of it LANDING? It pairs the live `PullRequest` count with how many of those PRs the merge sweep has refused `MERGE_STUCK_AFTER_TICKS`+ consecutive times, read off the `SweepSkipStreak` rows the sweep already maintains — two counts, no forge call, both slugs matched case-insensitively as everywhere else — and `MergeSignal.stalled` is the verdict: true only when every open PR is one of the refused ones. The consumer is `_issue_intake_scanner_for`, the single decision point for claiming NEW work: when every open PR is stuck it sets `can_claim = False`, reusing the heartbeat-only mode already there for a full budget, so in-flight claims keep their leases while nothing new is taken. It is deliberately NOT a `decide_admission` brake — ship and review are what CLEAR a backed-up pipeline, and braking them on the backlog they exist to drain would deadlock the factory against itself. Both directions are automatic: the brake applies on evidence and releases on evidence (one PR moving again suffices), with no operator step and no flag to reset; an unreadable probe reports `fresh=False` and never brakes; and a braked tick LOGS which PRs are stuck and how many, because a gate that declines quietly recreates the class of bug it exists to catch. **The verdict is per phase COST CLASS, never one answer for the whole queue ([#4098](https://github.com/souliane/teatree/issues/4098)).** The headless drain consulted the governor once and applied that single verdict to every pending row, so a 3-minute read-only `reviewing` task was refused on exactly the brake a 272-turn `coding` agent had caused — and reviewing and shipping are what RETIRE work (a merged PR frees a worktree and its agent), so refusing them alongside the class that filled the box removed the only available relief and the brake held itself on (measured 2026-08-03: 3h22m of denied admissions, zero review verdicts, 18 pending tasks, no merges). `core/modelkit/phases.CHEAP_PHASES` is the harness-owned taxonomy — the review lanes and the work-retiring `shipping`/`requesting_review` lanes — and `phase_cost` classifies anything unrecognised as EXPENSIVE, so a new or misspelled phase inherits the braked class rather than the exemption. CHEAP means **does not mutate source, and converges** (a review ends at a verdict, a ship at a merged PR), NOT that the agent is free: `_TOOLS_BY_PHASE` grants these phases `shell`, the cold-review procedure provisions a detached review checkout, a reviewer is told to run the suite, and a `shipping` push fires the parity hook. Because cheap still costs, the class is paired with a small ceiling rather than a free pass — the BOUND is what makes it safe, not the price of one member. `agent_admission_verdict` probes ONCE and resolves that probe per class (a drain costs one probe however many rows it walks, because nothing changes between iterations of that loop). Both cheap classes remain exempt from the MACHINE brake, while a spent token budget still refuses every class. The stored value is each class's width: 2 caps reviews and retiring work at two each inside a three-seat cheap lane, including at the machine brake. Reviews and retiring work are each bounded by `cheap_phase_admission_ceiling` (`UserSettings`, DB-home, per-overlay overridable, default **2**) inside a shared cheap lane one seat wider. Each class is bounded against its own occupancy — the running agents in that class plus its rows already handed to the runner (`Task.admitted_at`, taken through the one `AgentAdmission.admit` seam every chokepoint books its admission through, and released after `ADMITTED_INFLIGHT_WINDOW` so a dead runner cannot wedge the lane shut). That seat is what separate admission passes SHARE: a pass carries its headroom in memory only for its own span, so an in-memory count would have let a burst of passes admit past the ceiling one save at a time. **The seat is arbitrated INSIDE the write, not between the probe and it ([#4125](https://github.com/souliane/teatree/issues/4125)).** The occupancy read and the stamp were two unsynchronised statements, so two PROCESSES reaching a chokepoint in one window each saw room and each admitted — an effective bound of ceiling plus however many raced, which on a box that OOM-killed its worker three times in one evening is not free. `TaskQuerySet.record_admission` is therefore a conditional UPDATE whose `WHERE` re-checks the same class-specific `_lane_occupancy_q` predicate the probe counted (one predicate, two readers, so arbitration and probe cannot drift) and refuses a row that already holds a live seat; the loser matches no row and is refused rather than admitted. A conditional write rather than `select_for_update`, because the probe runs on every `post_save` of a waiting or terminal row and a row lock there would sit on a hot path. `admit` takes the seat BEFORE the dispatch, so a dispatch that then fails costs at most one `ADMITTED_INFLIGHT_WINDOW` of under-admission — the safe direction, and exactly what that window already recovers. A refusal already announced in the pass drops to DEBUG rather than repeating per held row, and a pass that finds released seats says so, because that is the state in which the ceiling is soft and it carries no refusal of its own to name it. Sharing the classification *and* durable class counts is what stops the exemption becoming the second unbounded lane #4097 cost. **Reviews are admitted outside the coding ceiling, in one ranked walk ([#5051](https://github.com/souliane/teatree/issues/5051)).** Reviewing must never be queued behind coding. The governor's ceiling bounds the EXPENSIVE lane alone, measured against `TaskQuerySet.expensive_lane_occupancy` — never the live population, which would refuse coding work because reviews are running. The review class (`REVIEW_PHASES`) is `agent_admission.review_lane_width()` wide BESIDE it — `cheap_phase_admission_ceiling`, floored at 1. The retiring class (`RETIRING_PHASES`: shipping and requesting_review) has the same width W as reviews inside a W+1 cheap lane, so two slow reviews cannot hold a push and two pushes can overlap. Both classes retain that width at the machine watermark; the token brake still applies. Background assessors (directive interpretation, short describe) gate no merge and stay in the coding lane; triage and news scanning use the bounded maintenance lane. `LaneWidths` carries the coding, review, retiring, answering, architectural-review and maintenance widths, so the worker's one executor per seat (`headless_lane_widths`, read every poll) gives every admitted review its own runner, and `t3 worker status` shows review and retiring occupancy beside the coding lane. Both headless chokepoints run ONE walk, `task_dispatch.admit_waiting_tasks`: the `post_save` receiver runs it when a row starts waiting and when a row reaches a terminal state (that save frees a seat), and the five-minute drain runs it as the safety net. The walk orders `TaskQuerySet.waiting_for_admission` (PENDING, due, holding no seat) by the shared `admission_priority` score, highest first: the sum of named parts — a priority part (`Ticket.expedited`, set with `t3 <overlay> ticket expedite`) and a stage part (review lane above started pull requests above continuing work above a new ticket's first phase). Started pull requests break equal-score ties by earliest current admission, oldest first; PR-less rows follow them, then task-row age breaks remaining ties. That yields expedited reviews, reviews, expedited work, started pull requests, continuing work, then new tickets. The loop claim, its spread and `t3 <overlay> tasks list` (`admission_score`) read the same score, so a further part (an owner-set priority, aging) is added in `admission_priority` alone. `CriticDispatch.enqueue` re-arms an expired claim only once its critic task has terminated, so a queued critic is never duplicated. The stall this prevents is still named rather than left invisible: `core/factory/drain_starvation.read_drain_lane_state` reports draining work queued, none of it running, past a 15-minute wait, and `t3 doctor check` WARNs on it (advisory). **Restore-side admission — a resume replays a staggered fleet in one step ([#4108](https://github.com/souliane/teatree/issues/4108)).** Background agents survive a harness restart: the harness restores them from their saved transcripts and resumes them, the whole set at once. The stagger the orchestrator applied was a property of the DISPATCH, not of the agents, so it is not replayed — an 8-core box went from healthy to load 58 with 1 GB free with no intervening dispatch decision by anyone. A restore is not a dispatch, so no dispatch-side ceiling can see it. `resume_agent_ceiling` is that path's own bound: `cores * HOST_AGENT_POPULATION_PER_CORE`, scaled by the load headroom still left under the same `BRAKE_LOAD_PER_CORE` watermark the dispatch lanes brake on, floored at 1 so a wedged box still gets the one agent that might unwedge it. It is a HOST population bound and deliberately NOT `admission_write_concurrency_per_core` — a per-lane concurrency setting bounds that lane and says nothing about how many agents the box is carrying in total, which is exactly the conflation the incident recorded (a lane capped at 3 while the two populations summed unchecked). `resume_shed_directive` returns the empty string at or under the ceiling, so a resume on an idle host is unchanged, and over it names the restored count AND the live ceiling it was judged against — the same never-silent contract every other refusal here keeps. The consumer is `hooks/scripts/resume_admission.py`, which counts the restored set as `<session>.agents` MINUS `<session>.agents-stopped` (the append-only dispatch ledger outlives its agents, so counting it alone would warn on history) and merges the directive into the ONE `SessionStart` write. Only `source == "resume"` is gated — a compaction is the same process and restores nothing. The resume guard reads Django-free only once the count is over the ceiling, so an idle resume adds no sqlite touch to the `SessionStart` hot path. **One normalised pressure scalar, and instrument only what a decision consumes ([#4508](https://github.com/souliane/teatree/issues/4508)).** The six brakes above were six independent `if`s, each with its own watermark and its own sentence, so the only expressible answers were *admit* and *refuse*: degradation was a cliff, and a box could read healthy on load while being out of weekly runway — the state the issue names. `core/admission_pressure.py` folds them into ONE value in which **1.0 IS each dimension's own existing watermark** (`weekly_utilization / WEEKLY_WINDOW_BRAKE`, `load1 / (BRAKE_LOAD_PER_CORE * cores)`, `1 + (RAM floor - available) / span`, `(1 - weekly_pace) / (1 - PACE_DENY)`, exhaustion as a flat 1.0), so the HALT verdict reproduces the pre-#4508 brake set exactly — pinned by a grid property test against a frozen oracle of the old predicates, asserting the **reason string** as well as the verdict. `BRAKE_PRECEDENCE` preserves the old ordered-`if` precedence among HALTing components, because exhaustion drives pace to zero and a numeric `max()` would silently rename a spent fleet after its derived symptom. The signal dataclasses (`QuotaSignal`, `MachineSignal`, `MachineBrake`) live in that module so the dependency runs one way, and `box_load_headroom` / `ram_headroom` come with them since each is exactly `1 -` its own component (#4125: one quantity, one reader). **What the fold BUYS is the range below 1.0, which six separate `if`s could not read.** Documented thresholds, and what degrades at each: **FULL** (`< 0.7`) nothing degrades; **DEGRADED** (`0.7-0.9`) intake concurrency tightens on the worst dimension — a continuous term, so this band names where it starts to bite rather than a new cliff; **SHED** (`>= admission_pressure_shed_at`, default 0.9) refuses the EXPENSIVE agent class while the CHEAP review/ship lanes that RETIRE work keep draining, the #4098 asymmetry restated (shedding the drain is the self-holding brake that cost 3h22m); **HALT** (`>= 1.0`) refuses every class, and in-flight work is never killed — only new admissions are refused, which is what "halt with partial results" means here. `decide_admission` is class-BLIND so it reads HALT only; `agent_admission_verdict` — the seam that already owns the cheap/expensive split — resolves SHED. **The token dimension finally reaches INTAKE.** `adapt_concurrency`'s occupancy term was load headroom alone, so a box with idle cores and free RAM kept claiming issues against a nearly-spent weekly window; it now takes `1 - pressure` (the parameter renamed `box_load_headroom` -> `admission_headroom`, because one that reads load-only while carrying the whole scalar is the stale name that made #4407 easy to miss). Because `1 -` the load component IS that old headroom, a CPU-bound box is byte-identical and the number moves only when quota is the worse dimension. Memory is deliberately WITHHELD from the scalar at that one call site: `additional` already owns "how many agents fit in memory", and two unsynchronised readers of one quantity drift. `resume_agent_ceiling` likewise keeps its two machine terms rather than the whole scalar — its consumer `hooks/scripts/resume_admission.py` is Django-free on the `SessionStart` hot path and the quota half needs the ORM. **No telemetry was added, because the second article's warning applies here literally** — `core/cycle_time/` already exists and nothing reads it ([#4480](https://github.com/souliane/teatree/issues/4480)). The scalar is computed at the decision point and discarded: no table, no span, no emitter, no migration. Every value has a named consumer before it is produced — `decide_admission` (HALT), `agent_admission_verdict` (SHED), `adapt_concurrency` via `IntakeConcurrencyScanner` (continuous), `t3 doctor check`'s box-occupancy line (the operator read), and the `_admission_pressure_signals` health collector. **Clustering by cause, not volume:** a braked factory refuses on every admission decision — hundreds of identical observations an hour — so that collector emits AT MOST ONE `HealthSignal` fingerprinted `admission-pressure:<dominant cause>`, and `KnownIssue.objects.record_signal` (which already dedupes on fingerprint) collapses N sightings into one row with `first_seen`/`last_seen` carrying the duration, auto-resolving when the pressure falls. Nothing is emitted below SHED: a factory merely under load is not an incident. **The metered lane gets a budget of its own ([#4816](https://github.com/souliane/teatree/issues/4816)).** The four quota components above are all Anthropic-SUBSCRIPTION — read from `AnthropicTokenUsage`, which the metered lane has no counterpart to — so a factory flipped onto that lane ran with no token dimension at all. `core/admission/metered_spend.py` sums `TaskAttempt` tokens on `lane=metered` over `metered_spend_window_hours` (default **24**) and normalises them against `metered_token_ceiling` (default **0** = UNSET, so the whole dimension is inert until an operator sets one — teatree cannot read a provider-side cycle limit and must not invent a budget). It is governed in TOKENS, never dollars: `cost_usd` on this lane is `cost_is_estimated` on every row, and a ceiling keyed on an estimate whose error is unknown is not a ceiling — the estimate is QUOTED in the refusal and labelled `ESTIMATED (price-table arithmetic, not a billed amount)`. No warn threshold is added: `spent / ceiling` is 1.0 AT the ceiling like every other dimension, so 0.9 already lands in the existing SHED band. The refusal also names how many attempts in the window recorded `usage_unknown`, calling the total a FLOOR, because an unreadable attempt contributes nothing to the sum. The ledger read lives in its own module rather than the governor, which is at its ten-public-function cap. **A budget only governs the lane that would spend it.** `core/admission/dispatch_lane.py` is the ONE authority on which Layer-2 lane a dispatch authenticates through: `LANE_BY_PROVIDER` moved out of `src/teatree/agents/runner.py`, so the governor judges a dispatch against the same lane the `TaskAttempt` is stamped with — a second copy of that mapping would be a second opinion about the very lane a refusal names. `agent_admission_verdict` resolves `configured_dispatch_lane()` once per probe (its signature unchanged) and selects the family: a METERED lane reads `MeteredSignal` and leaves `QuotaSignal` `fresh=False`; SUBSCRIPTION and the unattributed lane read the reverse. `fresh=False` ALREADY means "contributes no component", so "does not apply" needed no new mechanism. The two new components `metered-lane-parked` (a flat 1.0 while an uncleared metered `UsageWindowState` covers the lane — a provider that has already refused is harder evidence than any spend fraction) and `metered-spend` join `BRAKE_PRECEDENCE` after the subscription names and before `load`/`memory`; the families being mutually exclusive, the order between them never binds. Core may not import `teatree.agents` (tach), so the governor reads the PROVIDER pin rather than the harness registry — an unset pin means the ambient credential decided, which is genuinely unobservable from here, and the resulting unattributed lane keeps pre-#4816 behaviour byte-for-byte. A PER-OVERLAY lane is deliberately unmodelled: `agent_admission_verdict` is ONE probe per drain holding the `LaneBound` seat bookkeeping, and N per-overlay verdicts would each carry their own headroom and double-admit; `t3 doctor check` WARNs instead when registered overlays disagree on `agent_harness`. `cli/doctor/checks_metered_lane.py` carries that WARN and two siblings, each naming an unknown the governor cannot resolve for itself rather than being quietly wrong about it: a box riding the metered lane with `metered_token_ceiling` still 0 (the dimension is inert, so the lane is as unbounded as before), and attempts in the window carrying `usage_unknown` (they contribute nothing to the sum, so the measured spend is a FLOOR). All three are advisory like every sibling in `checks_admission_pressure` — an unset budget is an operator's choice, not a fault, and reddening the run on one trains the operator to ignore a red run. Rollback lever: `admission_pressure_shed_at` (`UserSettings`, DB-home, per-overlay overridable, default **0.9**), clamped on read into `[0.5, HALT_AT]` so a typo cannot wedge the expensive lane; `t3 <overlay> config_setting set admission_pressure_shed_at 1.0` collapses SHED into HALT and restores pre-#4508 admission byte-for-byte, while the governor continues to enforce the other pressure inputs. The quota brake applies on every admission decision, and load and memory pressure remain independent brakes. ONE switch for the family rather than one per lane — which lane a dispatch rides is not something the operator should have to know to turn the token brake off — and an unreadable setting keeps the brake ON, since a config read that raises must never widen admission. It is spent in `pressure_for`, the config half that owns it, by handing the fold the `UNREAD_QUOTA` that already contributes nothing: a flag only one caller would ever pass does not belong on the pure fold, and the ceiling is derived from the REAL quota beforehand, so standing the brake down refuses less without ever buying more concurrency. One boundary deliberately moved: pace alone denied at `< PACE_DENY` where the other five denied at `>=` their watermark, so the unified predicate now denies AT it — a measure-zero float boundary, in the conservative direction, with its own test naming the old operator.

When several machine components halt together, `load` and `memory` share one precedence rank: the largest normalised value names the refusal, and equal values retain the original order. Quota and metered causes still outrank both machine causes.

## Machine pressure on the host

**Machine pressure is read from `/proc` and the cgroup, each signal in its own scope.** `/proc/meminfo` and `/proc/loadavg` are host-wide inside every container, so the governor reads them directly and no feed carries them between processes. The host load average is judged against the HOST's cores (`MachineSignal.load_cores`, `os.cpu_count()`), while `cores` stays the CPUs the process can use for sizing. Memory is the host's `MemAvailable`, capped by the process's own cgroup headroom only when that cgroup is large enough to host an agent (`RamHeadroom.box_watermark_mib`): a small admin or webhooks container is never gated on its own budget, and the worker's reading carries its cgroup cap (`memory_cap_gb`). An unreadable `/proc/meminfo` is an unknown reading (`ram_available_gb is None`), never a cgroup number, and `memory_hold_reason` names it (`available memory unknown`). Swap and kernel memory pressure are not brakes: load and available memory already cover a thrashing box.

## Leaderless process groups

**Leaderless process groups — the load nobody owns and nothing reaps ([#4580](https://github.com/souliane/teatree/issues/4580)).** The governor above throttles on `os.getloadavg()`, and load average counts *runnable* threads rather than CPU — so a process group whose leader has exited steals admitted capacity while reading healthy on every per-process metric. One did: 37 shells from a dead fuzz run, niced and mostly sleeping at ~0.0% CPU, held the box for 9 days 10 hours and removed ~58% of admitted capacity. `core/cleanup/orphan_process_groups.py` is the reader — leader gone, oldest member past `orphan_group_min_age_hours` (default **6**), and still burning (a runnable member, or a sustained rate over 0.05 cores; a single instantaneous sample is a coin flip on a group that is mostly in `S`). It reads through `core/cleanup/process_table.py`, the one process-table seam, and the two tables are NOT interchangeable: this venue's `/proc` numbers pids this process may signal, while `/host-proc` numbers them in the host's namespace where the same integer names a different process here — those are report-only, partitioned off the venue scan by `NSpid` so a container process visible in both is never counted twice. Two surfaces consume it: `_check_orphaned_process_groups` (an advisory `t3 doctor check` WARN beside `_check_box_occupancy`, which saw this surplus and could not explain it) and `t3 tool reap-orphan-groups`, dry-run by default, SIGTERM only, refusing a non-signalable group, `pgid <= 1`, its own group or an ancestor's, and any member matching the never-reap list. Reaping stays an explicit operator action rather than a loop lever.

## Started pull request priority

The shared admission score places a ticket with a live pull request above unstarted work in its lane: 350 for a writing phase versus 100 for ordinary continuing work, and 900 versus 600 in the review lane. A new planning task waiting at least 24 hours rises from 0 to 150, ahead of lower-priority continuing work. The 200-point expedite part remains additive, while every review stage still exceeds an expedited writing stage. The same score orders due claims and pending-task visibility, including parked rows. Within equal scores, started pull requests use the ticket’s earliest current `Task.admitted_at`, falling back to its first transition and then to its pull request’s creation stamp, oldest first. The pull request creation stamp bounds a renewed admission that is later than the pull request; clearing a seat can lose the original admission time. Other tasks keep their task-row age order. The `PullRequest` ledger defines live as neither merged nor closed, and the same clock drives the start-to-merged signal.

Live pull request rows set the started-work admission stage and feed the merge-brake diagnosis. Task claims require pending status and a due `not_before` deadline; lane ceilings and machine pressure bound admission seats. The merge brake gates new issue intake.

The tickets loop rechecks pending tasks older than 24 hours. A per-task timestamp in `Ticket.extra['last_validated_at']` bounds checks to once per day without a migration. The scanner walks past already-stamped rows, checks at most 50 unstamped tasks, and starts no further checks after its 10-second tick budget. It reads the issue, recorded target branch, and branch-content verdict; plan currency is checked only for coding tasks on a ticket at `PLAN_RECORDED`, where the FSM applies that gate. A changed or unreadable premise is appended to the task reason and ticket context. A closed issue or already-landed branch supersedes a coding task; later phases retain their pending task and receive a note. A stale coding plan queues a fresh planning task. Unreadable external evidence never cancels work. The cycle-time page reports the median and maximum wait from a completed phase's last attempt to the next phase's admission, including the current age of a pending next phase.

Identical checkout parks double the retry from 30 minutes to a 24-hour cap; from the third with no known holder, one health row names the halt. A daily scanner retries the checkout fence for pending writing tasks without admitting an agent. When the fence passes, it re-arms the task and clears the halt; a stamped task that is no longer pending or has been superseded also clears its stale halt. A known holder's release continues to re-arm its own parked tasks immediately.

The dead-worktree scanner classifies registry rows on an hourly cadence and records one durable health row per kept disposition; the render phase reads those rows without running another git probe. An unreadable memory sample holds provision and self-improve admission with a named unknown reason.

## Shared pytest worker slots

**Shared pytest worker slots (#5110).** `quality.pytest_resource_contract.WorkerSlotPool` holds one cross-process file lock per xdist worker under `TEATREE_PYTEST_WORKER_POOL_ROOT/pytest-worker-slots`; without the dedicated variable, it uses `loop_registry_dir()` as the root. Participants coordinate a single capacity in `capacity.lock`, growing it to the largest observed CPU allowance times `TOTAL_TEST_WORKERS_PER_CORE` so a smaller container can use every free slot. The factor is 1. The governor's per-agent value and any explicit `-n` are requests, not grants. A distributed controller acquires slots in `pytest_sessionstart` before xdist starts nodes, then reduces `config.option.tx` and `numprocesses` to the granted count. A multiplied `--tx N*spec` leases all N workers; collection and non-distributed runs take no slot. A creatable shared root is created; an unreadable or corrupt capacity file is rebuilt from the participant's capacity under the root lock; a root or slot that cannot be created, opened or locked refuses startup with its path. An exhausted pool logs its path, capacity and holder and waits; a partial grant prints `granted N of M`. Closing the lock descriptors at pytest teardown or process death returns slots. A live parent lease is inherited by a nested pytest run. CI uses `/tmp` as its job-local shared root, while production containers use the host bind mount. An explicit FULL verdict from `dev/test-affected.sh` carries `--teatree-selected-full`; bare unsharded whole-tree calls fail before collection. Direct local package-directory selections above 1,200 recorded test seconds, or without coverage in an existing durations file, fail before collection. A missing durations file keeps the path-only guard for mutation copies; CI and declared curated runs retain their existing routes.

If a pytest controller is SIGKILLed, its locks release immediately while orphaned xdist workers may continue briefly.

**An owner's Slack question has its own admission class ([#5655](https://github.com/souliane/teatree/issues/5655)).** `phase_cost("answering")` is `PhaseCost.ANSWERING`: not the coding (expensive) complement, and not a share of the review and retiring seats ([#5051](https://github.com/souliane/teatree/issues/5051)). The class holds at most `ANSWERING_LANE_WIDTH` seats (2, harness-owned like the cheap membership, no setting). They are booked by the same conditional write as the other lanes (`record_admission(..., lane=PhaseCost.ANSWERING)`) and counted by `TaskQuerySet.lane_occupancy(PhaseCost.ANSWERING)`; the expensive occupancy excludes answering rows, so answering never counts against the coding ceiling, and a flood of answering rows takes at most its own width, leaving coding, review and retiring their seats. Like the cheap classes it is exempt from the machine-load, memory and shed brakes; the token brake and a claim block still refuse it, at admission and at claim (`claim_denied_reason` applies the cheap guard to it). The width comes from a measurement of the answering run: a median of 12.5 turns and a mean cost of 0.63 USD against 49 turns and 5.53 USD (mean) for coding, an agent turn of 18 s at the median (p90 27 s, max 49 s) that starts about 3 s after the seat, and at most two answering tasks created inside any 60 s window. Answering rows run on the `cheap` queue and the worker adds `ANSWERING_LANE_WIDTH` threads to it, so every seated row has a thread and the machine brake spares them with the other `cheap` executors. The owner's acceptance bound is that the first reply to his Slack question arrives in under 60 seconds, measured from his message to the first reply in the thread (the number is the owner's); the first text reply to a fresh unbound question is the answer itself, so the budget is the inbound wake, one answer cycle, admission and the answering run.

## Box-global load settings

The brake and resume factors are global ConfigSetting values. Writes accept finite values from 0.5 to 6.0 per core; the live reader keeps the shipped 2.0/1.5 pair if the stored pair loses its hysteresis or cannot be read. `t3 doctor check` reports the effective brake watermark. The shed setting may go down to 0.5, so an operator can stop new expensive starts before the 0.7 degrade band during a burst; 1.0 still collapses SHED into HALT.
